An all clear is a guess.

Most blur apps hand you a soft smear and a good feeling. BleepFace attacks its own work. Check sharpens, sampled frame by sampled frame, the way a stranger with free tools would, and tells you when a face comes back.

Not safe. A recognizable face was recovered on 15 of 15 sampled frames. This redaction can be reversed. Use a stronger blur or Blackout.

When nothing comes back, it says exactly that, and nothing more. It will never tell you a video is safe, because no software honestly can. Check is free, forever, and nothing on that screen can be saved or shared.

Blur it any way.

Every blur stays editable right up until you export. Stretch it, move it, restyle it, or take it off.

  • BlurSoft cover with an edge you set from hard to soft.
  • PixelsClassic mosaic, sized to the face.
  • ScrambleShuffled blocks that do not resolve into a face.
  • BlackoutA solid panel. The one that beats everything.
  • FlareA bright bloom for a lighter touch.
  • EmojiAny emoji or sticker riding on the face, with a cover underneath or nothing at all.
  • TimingSet in and out points by scrubbing the footage. Nudge by fractions of a second.
  • ShapesStretch a blur into a rectangle for plates, screens, and signs.

Processed on your iPhone.

Face detection and blurring happen on the device in your hand. Photos and videos are processed on this iPhone and never uploaded.

  • No cloud. There is no server, so there is nothing to upload to.
  • No account. No sign in, no email, no profile.
  • No analytics. No tracking SDKs, no ad networks, no third parties.
  • Your library, your call. The app only sees the videos you pick and only saves what you export.
